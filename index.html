How I tend to work

This is how I approach almost everything I do, be that design, development, team process, strategy, carpentry or figuring out life.

🕵️ Analysis & understanding Understanding how things, systems and people work and interact, and how they ended up that way is my favourite place to start, because otherwise it's so much harder to figure out how to change and improve things. I do this from a lens of user research, anthropology, systems thinking and "artifact analysis" (e.g. looking at tech stacks, processes and what's already there).
💡 Ideation & approaches Brainstorming and exploring solution spaces with a diverse set of perspectives and approaches is good stuff, especially when you figure out win-win situations, solutions that will improve over time, or managing to get around problems altogether.
🧑‍🔬 Prototyping & testing Since we can never know if and how something will work in practice, the best way to figure it out is to just try. Sketching, prototyping, experimenting, scaffolding and testing things in real life, with real users or teams or APIs or whathaveyou, is the best way to learn what works.
🛠️ Implementation & adoption Actually implementing and building things out is where I often prefer to collaborate with others in a group where we can complement each other, and focus on simplicity, understandability and usability of what is built. I love working with early adopters and the exchange of knowledge that makes things better for everyone.
♾️ Rinse & repeat Things are always changing and impacting other things, so there's always new ideas to explore, new challenges that arise, new problems to be solved and new boxes to think outside of.

When I thrive

Because I'm drawn to points of friction and want to understand and explain problems and challenges I see around me, I thrive in environments where it's possible and encouraged to have open conversations, where questions, curiosity and new perspectives are welcomed, and where it's possible or encouraged to leave your lane. I've been told I do this with "natural ego-less honesty", probably because I think people generally want to do the best they can and we often want the same outcomes, but might have different ideas for how to get there.
